01 What are Cleft Sentences?

A cleft sentence (from the word "cleft" meaning split) splits a standard statement into two clauses, placing a spotlight on the most crucial piece of information.

What + Subject + Verb + is / was + ★ Focused Info
Natural I love my job.
Cleft What I love is my job.

By moving the core info to the end of the sentence, you draw the listener’s absolute attention to it. This technique is highly valued in essays, formal presentations, and storytelling.

Spotlight focus: like a single poppy flower ✿
02 Cleft Sentence Patterns

Depending on the tone and focus, choose one of these three standard structural frames:

1. What-clause (Most common)
Emphasis placed after the verb BE.
What I appreciate most is your honesty.
2. The thing (that)
A slightly more formal version.
The thing that impressed me was his dedication.
3. What... about... is...
Perfect to show specific context.
What I love about that website is the photos.
✏️ Remember: The information coming at the very end of a cleft pattern receives 100% of the conversational spotlight!
Write it down to remember! 📖
03 Inversion for Emphasis

Inversion changes the standard order of words. Placing a negative or restrictive adverbial first forces the auxiliary verb to jump in front of the subject.

Negative Word + Auxiliary (did/have/do) + Subject + Verb...
Natural He had never seen such beauty.
Inverted Never had he seen such beauty.

Primary Restrictive Triggers:

Never Rarely Only then Hardly At no time Not once No sooner In no way
Flipped order: like leaves in the wind 🍂
04 Mistakes & Traps

Avoid these common mistakes to keep your sentences grammatically sound:

Trap 1 Forgetting the subject-auxiliary flip after "Only".
✗ Only then I understood the rule.
✔ Only then did I understand the rule.
Trap 2 Forgetting the auxiliary verb entirely.
✗ Never he has seen anything like it.
✔ Never has he seen anything like it.
Trap 3 Adding prepositional noise into clean Cleft frames.
✗ What I love about is the photos.
✔ What I love about that website is the photos.
💡 Mnemonic checklist:
1. Look at the trigger.
2. Put the auxiliary.
3. Put the subject.
4. Put the verb.
