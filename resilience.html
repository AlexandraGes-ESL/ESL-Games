Reading

The Personality Problem

The staff at Paws & Co. Animal Shelter had a problem. They had twelve cats, twelve very different personalities, and a waiting list of families who all wanted "a nice, friendly cat." The trouble was that not every cat agreed on what "nice" meant.

Take Cleo. She was assertive — she knew exactly what she wanted. If you opened a tin of tuna anywhere in the building, she appeared within seconds.

Then there was Biscuit — generous, patient, and completely reliable. If a new kitten arrived feeling frightened, Biscuit would always sit beside them. He simply waited until they were ready.

The most eccentric resident was Professor Whiskers. He predicted the weather by watching dust. If the dust moved left, he would hide under the sofa. He was wrong approximately 80% of the time, but this did not seem to bother him.

The newest arrival, a shy tabby named Pixel, had not left her box in three days. The staff were thoughtful. If they had rushed her, she would have become even more anxious. Instead, on the fourth day, she walked out on her own.

By the end of the month, all cats found homes. Sometimes the most impulsive families chose the most laid-back cats, and somehow it worked perfectly.
