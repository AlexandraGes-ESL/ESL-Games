What you will do

Three structures, eleven edits, one published page. Every choice is judged by credibility — a desk that prints wild claims loses its reputation.

No score for speed. Only for meaning.

Звук включается после первого клика.

Three structures

It is said that…
She is said to be…
She is said to have been…

Why passive?

Because the writer does not want to name the source. That is the whole point of the construction.

01 · Notice

Same fact. Two very different sentences.

A fan posts at 2:41 a.m.

ok so apparently she dated a vampire back in 2019?? nobody has receipts but everyone’s saying it — anonymous, fan forum, 2:41 a.m.

Your desk publishes this.

She is said to have dated a vampire in 2019.

Look closely. Three things changed. Which one matters most for the meaning?

Key idea

This construction exists so you can pass on a claim without owning it and without naming the source.

Where you meet it

Wikipedia, news reports, legal statements, science writing: It is estimated that…

02 · The three shapes

Pick the shape that matches the claim.

Shape A · the general claim
it + passive reporting verb + that + clause

It is often said that carrots help you see in the dark.
It was once believed that the Earth was flat.

Use when you report a general view, not one person’s words.

Shape B · same time
subject + passive reporting verb + (not) to-infinitive

Carrots are said to help you see in the dark.
The Earth was once thought to be flat.

The verb refers to the same time as the reporting verb.

Shape C · earlier action
subject + passive reporting verb + to have + past participle

Columbus is often believed to have been Spanish.
The fairies were proven not to have existed.

The action happened before the reporting verb.

NOW or THEN? The only question that matters

With shapes B and C the whole choice is about when the claim is true, not when you are writing.

If the claim is true…UseExample
now — at the same time as the reportto be
to be + -ing		She is said to be in Reykjavík.
She is said to be recording an album.
then — earlier than the reportto have + past participle
to have been + -ing		She is said to have dated a vampire in 2019.
She is said to have been recording there last year.
then — and it was a stateto have beenColumbus is believed to have been Spanish.
Test it with two words. Add now and in 2019 to the sentence in your head.
True now → to be. True back then → to have been / to have done.
The reporting verb is independent. You can write It is said (people still say it) about an event in 2019 — It is said that she dated a vampire in 2019. The reporting verb shows whether the talking is happening now; the infinitive shows when the event was.
Do not add the source. The construction exists to avoid it.
✓ It is widely believed that… ✗ It is believed by many people that…
Negative goes before to: were proven not to have existed, not to not have existed.
Useful reporting verbs: believe, claim, consider, estimate, expect, feel, hope, know, prove, report, rumour, say, show, think, understand.
Choose by evidence: proven needs proof. Rumoured means there is none. Estimated fits numbers.

Коротко по-русски: A — общее мнение, B — действие в то же время, C — действие раньше. Источник не называем никогда, отрицание ставим перед to.

04 · Newsroom

Now say it out loud.

The desk has three live situations. Pick one, take 60 seconds, and report the claim without naming a source. Use the shapes you just practised.

1. The press briefing

You are the studio spokesperson

Journalists ask about the leaked plot. You cannot confirm anything and cannot name who told you.

  • What is said to be in the final scene?
  • What did the director reportedly refuse to do in 2023?
  • What is the studio believed to have spent on the trailer?
It is understood that…
The director is said to have…
The budget is believed to be…
The cast is not expected to…

2. The correction desk

You are fixing a careless article

Yesterday your site printed three claims as facts. Say them again, but this time as careful, distanced statements.

  • She stole the demo tape. (no proof)
  • He was born in Madrid. (he died in 1506)
  • The band broke up in 2019. (they are touring now)
She is rumoured to have…
He is believed to have been…
The band is said to be…

3. The interview

You are the fan being interviewed

A podcast asks what the fandom believes. Report the rumours honestly — and show which ones you do not believe.

  • What does everyone think about the album?
  • What is the most ridiculous rumour?
  • Which claim was proven false?
It is widely believed that…
It is often claimed that…, but…
The rumour was proven not to…
1:00 Speak without stopping. Mistakes are fine — keep going.

Rule of the room

Never say by people, fans say, or everyone thinks. The whole point is that you do not name the source.

Push yourself

Use at least one to have + past participle. If everything you say is to be, you are stuck in the present.

Reflection

After 60 seconds, ask yourself: which claim did I make sound stronger than my evidence?

04 · Your article

Write the page yourself.

Now the real work. Below is a rumour thread. Write four encyclopaedic lines using all three shapes.

1. her new album?? it’s been “coming soon” for two years lol
2. someone said she turned down the film role back in 2023
3. no proof, but people think the song is about her ex
4. apparently she’s recording in Reykjavík right now
5. and the 2021 “beef”?? never even happened — fan thread, unverified

Before you publish, check yourself:

Model page
Her new album is said to have been in production for two years.
It is reported that she turned down the film role in 2023.
The song is rumoured to be about her ex-boyfriend.
She is understood to be recording in Reykjavík at the moment.
The 2021 disagreement is widely believed not to have happened.

Target language

is said to have / is believed to be / it is thought that / is rumoured to / were proven not to have

Style rule

If you cannot prove it, lower the claim: rumoured, reportedly, claimed to.
